Bio

Nilan is a Town & Country Planning graduate from the University of Moratuwa, with a background in GIS, remote sensing, spatial modelling, and urban analytics. His research interests include geospatial data science, outdoor thermal comfort, microclimate analysis, and spatial optimisation, with a focus on supporting climate-aware tourism planning and destination management.

Research project

Optimising Pedestrian-Level Sensor Locations for Representative Outdoor Thermal Comfort Monitoring at Selected Sri Lankan Tourist Destinations

This research aims to develop and evaluate a geospatial approach for selecting a minimum set of monitoring locations that represents the diversity of outdoor thermal environments experienced by tourists. Focusing on tourist walking routes and outdoor activity areas, the proposed approach balances tourist exposure with coverage of substantially distinct microclimates. Temporary field measurements, spatial environmental characteristics, and observed visitor-use patterns will inform the selection and assessment of representative sensor locations. The findings are expected to support efficient thermal comfort monitoring and climate-aware destination management.

Thesis work

PhD research on spatial optimisation of outdoor thermal comfort monitoring locations in Sri Lankan tourist destinations.

The research involves reviewing relevant literature, characterising microclimatic diversity and tourist exposure, developing a sensor-location optimisation framework, and evaluating selected locations through temporary field campaigns. Current work focuses on refining the proposal, establishing destination-selection criteria, and designing a feasible measurement and evaluation strategy within available equipment and fieldwork constraints.

Research project

Translating Asian Elephant Movement Behaviour into an Agent-Based Model for Land-Use Decision-Making in Human–Elephant Conflict Landscapes: A Case Study Approach

This research focuses on translating existing knowledge of Asian elephant movement behaviour across ecological and human-modified landscapes into an agent-based model to support land-use decision-making in human–elephant conflict landscapes. Using Galgamuwa, Sri Lanka, as the case study area, the research involves compiling and organising existing behavioural knowledge, developing an agent-based model to represent elephant decision-making in contested environments, and evaluating its utility as a decision-support tool for spatial planning and land-use management.

Thesis work

The thesis carries the same title as the research project. The first two research objectives have been completed, and work is currently focused on the third objective.

  • Objective 1 (completed): To compile and organise existing knowledge on wild elephant behaviour across ecological and human-modified landscapes.
  • Objective 2 (completed): To develop an agent-based model that translates behavioural knowledge into a framework for understanding decision-making in contested environments.
  • Objective 3 (in progress): To appraise the utility of the developed model as a decision-support tool for spatial planning and land-use decision-making.

Research project

Perceived Safety and Settlement Morphology in Contested Landscapes: Human–Elephant Coexistence in Sri Lanka’s Dry Zone

Human–elephant conflict (HEC) in Sri Lanka’s Dry Zone presents challenges for rural livelihoods, community safety, and settlement planning. This research investigates how settlement characteristics and lived experiences shape perceived safety in HEC-affected communities in the North Western and North Central provinces. Using qualitative interviews, questionnaires, field observations, thematic analysis, and spatial interpretation, it also examines traditional tank-based village morphology to identify spatial principles for safer human–elephant coexistence.

Thesis work

The thesis carries the same title as the research project. It is currently at the synthesis and writing stage, consolidating research findings, analysis, and the identified spatial principles into the final thesis. A draft thesis will be submitted ahead of the final submission, which is scheduled for 10 December 2026.
