Research Interests

01

Urban heat and sustainable urban planning

This research area examines the thermal behaviour of tropical urban environments and its implications for sustainable urban planning. Land surface temperature (LST) distributions are assessed with satellite remote sensing and spatial statistics, while field experiments capture the effect of specific urban surfaces and street elements on the near-surface thermal environment. Areas characterized by dense built structures tend to exhibit elevated temperatures, consistent with the urban heat island phenomenon. Conversely, green spaces and street trees appear to provide a mitigative effect, the magnitude of which likely depends on their configuration and placement.

Holistically, the work attempts to translate these observations into evidence that can inform the design of cooler and more liveable urban spaces.

Methods and tools

  • Remote sensing
  • GIS
  • Spatial autocorrelation
  • Field experiments

02

Geohazard prediction for geoengineered structures

Geoengineered structures such as earth dams, landfill capping systems and drainage layers are increasingly exposed to climate-induced loading. This research area employs machine learning and statistical modelling to assess and forecast the response of such structures, including probabilistic stability evaluation of wildlife-damaged earth dams, neural-network forecasting of moisture dynamics within capping systems, and hybrid artificial intelligence approaches for interface shear strength. The physical processes governing these responses, however, remain only partly understood, which is reflected in the attention given to the fundamentals of internal instability in soils.

To this end, data-driven predictions are examined alongside the underlying mechanics, with the aim of narrowing the gap in predicting geohazards and risk.

Methods and tools

  • Machine learning
  • Neural networks
  • Probabilistic analysis
  • Geotechnical engineering

Related publications

03

Geo-environmental remediation and nature-based solutions

This research area focuses on environmental remediation techniques that rely on natural processes, with particular attention to floating wetlands for the treatment of Acid Mine Drainage (AMD). Nature-based water purification and ecological restoration offer a low-cost alternative to conventional treatment, although their performance is likely to vary with site conditions and seasonal dynamics. The work draws on a mining and civil engineering background to examine how such systems can be integrated into optimized geo-environmental design aimed at combating climate change.

Methods and tools

  • Environmental remediation
  • Floating wetlands
  • Geo-environmental design
